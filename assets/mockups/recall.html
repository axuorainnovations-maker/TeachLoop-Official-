What does the term 'Cold War' specifically refer to, and why is it called 'cold' rather than 'hot'?

Model Answer

The Cold War was the ideological and geopolitical conflict between the United States and the Soviet Union from roughly 1947 to 1991, characterized by tension, proxy wars, and nuclear brinkmanship but no direct military confrontation between the superpowers. It's called 'cold' because despite intense hostility, it never escalated into direct warfare between the two nuclear-armed nations.
